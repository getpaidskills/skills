# Regional notes (informational, not legal advice)

These are pointers to what lawyers typically ask about. They are deliberately short; the user's
lawyer decides what applies.

## EU / UK — GDPR, UK GDPR, consumer law
- Need a lawful basis for each processing purpose (contract, legitimate interests, consent, legal
  obligation). Privacy policy must list purposes, recipients, retention, rights, transfers outside
  the EU/UK and how to complain to a supervisory authority.
- Non-essential cookies (analytics, ads) generally require prior consent (ePrivacy rules).
  Cookieless analytics avoid the banner in many cases — confirm with counsel.
- Digital content: 14-day withdrawal right; lost once delivery starts only with the consumer's
  express consent + acknowledgement at checkout.
- Businesses outside the EU that target EU users may need an EU representative (GDPR Art. 27) —
  ask a lawyer whether the exemption for occasional, low-risk processing applies.
- Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj

## California — CCPA / CPRA
- Applies to for-profit businesses meeting revenue or data-volume thresholds; many small indie
  products are below them, but including the rights section is low cost.
- If you "sell" or "share" personal information (incl. cross-context behavioural advertising) you
  need a "Do Not Sell or Share" link. The template states you do not — make sure that is true
  (ad pixels may count as sharing).
- https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa

## Mainland China — PIPL
- Separate consent is required for certain processing, including cross-border transfers of
  personal information, and additional mechanisms (standard contract, certification or security
  assessment) may apply. Sites hosted in mainland China also need ICP filing.
- The template only adds a placeholder section; get local advice before serving mainland users.

## Children
- US COPPA applies to services directed at under-13s; EU member states set digital consent ages
  between 13 and 16. The template defaults to 16.
