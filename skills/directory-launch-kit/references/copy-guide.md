# Copy guide

Every word in the kit comes from the product and the user. These rules keep it within each form's
limits and keep reviewers and readers on your side.

## Tagline (≤ 60 characters, Product Hunt and most launchpads)

- Say what it does for whom: "Invoices that send and chase themselves", not "The future of billing".
- No product name (it is shown next to it), no emoji strings, no "AI-powered" unless that is the point.
- Check the count: `make_kit.py` prints `n/60`.

## Description (≤ 260 for short fields, ≤ 500 for Product Hunt)

Three sentences: what it is, the one thing it does better, the price. Example:
"Acme turns a tracked hour into a finished invoice, sends it, and follows up until it is paid. Built
for freelancers who would rather do the work than the admin. Free for 3 clients, $9/month after."

Never write: user counts, revenue, "trusted by", star ratings, press logos or rankings the user can't
show; "cheapest" or "best" comparisons; claims about a competitor.

## Show HN title (≤ 80 characters)

`Show HN: <Name> – <what it does, plainly>`. HN readers dislike marketing words; describe the thing.
Show HN rules (news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html): people must be able to try it, ideally without a
signup; no landing pages or fundraisers; you must be the maker and around to discuss it; never ask
anyone to upvote or comment.

## The maker comment

Post it yourself right after submitting on Product Hunt, Indie Hackers or Reddit. Shape:
1. Who you are and why you built it (one or two sentences, true).
2. What it does today (three bullets, no roadmap promises).
3. What you want feedback on (one specific question).
4. The price, plainly.

The agent drafts this **with** the user; the user edits and posts it.

## Chinese communities (V2EX 分享创造, 即刻)

Write in Chinese, as the maker, plainly: 做了什么、为什么做、怎么用、价格. Don't cross-post the same text
to many nodes; answer comments.

## Agent-skill marketplaces

Lead with the job the skill does and the agents it works in (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor). Link the
repo, show the install command, keep the README's first screen honest about what is free and paid.
